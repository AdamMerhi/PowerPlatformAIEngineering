# How Motor Insurance Works — Claims Handling Primer

> **Synthetic background document** for the Claims PDS Agent POC. It explains general principles in plain language.
> It is **not** part of the policy. Coverage and eligibility are decided only by the numbered clauses of the Product
> Disclosure Statement (PDS). Never cite this primer as the reason to accept or decline a claim.

Each section below is self-contained so it can be embedded and retrieved on its own.

## P1. What insurance is

**P1.1 The basic idea.** The insured pays a premium. In return the insurer agrees to pay for specific kinds of loss, described in the policy, if they happen during the policy period. The insurer pays only what the policy promises, no more and no less.

**P1.2 Indemnity.** Insurance puts the customer back in the position they were in before the loss. It does not make them better off. For a vehicle, the most the insurer pays is the vehicle's market value, less the excess.

**P1.3 Excess.** The excess is the part of each claim the customer pays. The insurer pays the amount above it. A claim at or below the excess pays nothing.

## P2. What a claim is

**P2.1 The customer's job.** The customer reports the loss promptly, describes it honestly and provides the information needed to assess it.

**P2.2 The insurer's job.** The insurer reads the claim, checks it against the policy and decides fairly and promptly. It must tell the customer the decision and the reasons.

**P2.3 Policy period.** A claim is only payable for a loss that happened between the policy start date and end date. Cover is not backdated.

## P3. How a claim is assessed

**P3.1 Facts first, then the policy.** An assessor first establishes the facts (what was insured, who was driving, when and how the loss happened, how much is claimed). Then they check each fact against the policy terms that apply.

**P3.2 Three possible outcomes.** A claim is accepted when every applicable term is met. It is declined when a specific term is not met. It is referred when the policy says a person must look closer before a decision is made, or when a fact needed for the decision is missing.

**P3.3 Referral is not a decision.** A referred claim has not been accepted or declined. The next step is the review the policy requires, for example an assessor, a senior assessor, or confirmation of a missing fact.

**P3.4 Short or unusual policies.** Where a policy was in force for only part of a year, insurers often look more closely, because there is less history to rely on. The policy sets the exact threshold.

## P4. Declining a claim fairly

**P4.1 A decline must rest on a policy term.** An insurer can decline only by pointing to a term that is actually in the policy and that applies to the facts. If no such term exists, the claim cannot be declined on that basis.

**P4.2 Give the clause and the reason.** A declined customer must be told which clause was relied on and why it applies, in plain language.

**P4.3 Never invent an exclusion.** Relying on an exclusion that the policy does not contain, or on a term that does not apply to the facts, is a serious conduct failure. Regulators have taken action against insurers that declined valid claims this way. The remedy is to re-check the decision against the actual policy wording.

**P4.4 Unsupported declines are escalated.** If a second check cannot find a policy term that supports a decline, the decision is not final until a more senior person has reviewed it.

## P5. People and checks

**P5.1 A person decides.** Software can summarise a claim and suggest an outcome. A claims officer makes the decision and is accountable for it.

**P5.2 Independent second check.** Good practice is that a decision is checked a second time, independently, against the policy. If the two disagree, the disagreement is shown and a line manager approves the final outcome.

**P5.3 Keep the record.** Every decision records the clauses relied on, who decided and when, so it can be reviewed later.
